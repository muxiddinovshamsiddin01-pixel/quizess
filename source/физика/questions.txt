Question 13
A conservative system goes from state A to state B and the change in the potential energy is
equal to 200 J. Which of the following statements is correct?
(A) The variation of kinetic energy is 400 J
(B) The variation of kinetic energy is 200 J
(C) The work of the forces is equal to −200 J
(D) The work of the forces is equal to 200 J
Apply conservation of mechanical energy
∆Etotal = ∆K + ∆U = 0 ⇒∆K = −∆U = −200 J
Use work-energy principle
Since the system is conservative, the work done by conservative forces is:
W = ∆K = −200 J
Final Answer: (C) The work of the forces is equal to -200 J
Concepts and Laws
• In conservative systems, the total mechanical energy (K + U) is constant.
• A gain in potential energy means an equal loss in kinetic energy.
• The work done by conservative forces is always equal to ∆K.

Question 113

(A) No process can produce more work than the heat it accepts.
(B) No engine can produce more work than the heat it intakes.
(C) An engine cannot produce work without accepting heat.
(D) An engine has to reject heat.
The Kelvin–Planck statement of the second law of thermodynamics asserts:
“It is impossible for any device operating in a cycle to convert all the heat it receives from a
single reservoir into an equivalent amount of work.”
This means:
• A heat engine must reject part of the absorbed heat to a sink.
• It cannot convert 100% of heat into work without dumping some heat.
Option (D) expresses this core idea most accurately: An engine has to reject heat.
Final Answer: (D)
Concepts and Laws
• Kelvin–Planck Statement (Second Law): No cyclic heat engine can completely con-
vert heat from a single source into work.
• Cyclic Heat Engine Operation: Heat input ⇒partial conversion to work ⇒remaining
heat rejected to a sink.
• Thermal Efficiency Limitation: All real engines must expel some heat, reducing ideal
efficiency.

Question 114
Select an incorrect statement relating to a Carnot cycle.
(A) There are two adiabatic processes.
(B) There are two constant-pressure processes.
(C) Work occurs for all four processes.
(D) Each process is a reversible process.
The Carnot cycle consists of:
• Two isothermal processes (constant temperature):
– Heat is added at high temperature.
– Heat is rejected at low temperature.
• Two adiabatic processes (no heat exchange):
– Expansion and compression connecting the two isotherms.
There are no constant-pressure processes in the Carnot cycle. Therefore, statement (B) is
incorrect.
Final Answer: (B)
Concepts and Laws
• Carnot Cycle: Theoretical cycle that represents the maximum efficiency possible for a
heat engine.
• Processes involved:
– Two reversible isothermal processes.
– Two reversible adiabatic processes.
• Key Properties:
– All processes are reversible.
– Work is done during all processes (positive or negative).
– No constant-pressure or constant-volume steps in the ideal Carnot cycle.

Internal energy U and entropy S are both state functions in thermodynamics. This means
their values depend only on the current state of the system (e.g., temperature, pressure,
volume), not on the path taken to reach that state.
In contrast to path functions (like work or heat), state functions have well-defined values
at each equilibrium state.
(A): ”both depend only on the temperature”: Not true — internal energy and entropy can
also depend on other variables like volume or pressure, depending on the system.
(B) and (D) are identical and incorrect: ”both do not depend on the temperature; their vari-
ations are always greater than zero”. This is false — both can depend on temperature, and
their variations are not always greater than zero. For instance, entropy can decrease in a
system (if heat flows out), though the total entropy of the universe must increase.
Both U and S are state functions, so the correct choice is (C).
Final Answer: (C)
Concepts and Laws
• Internal Energy U: Represents the total energy stored in the system (microscopically
as kinetic + potential energies of particles).
• According to the 1st Law of Thermodynamics: ∆U = Q −W
Where: Q = heat added to the system, W = work done by the system
• U depends on variables like temperature, volume, and number of particles — not
just temperature.
• Entropy S: A measure of disorder or the number of accessible microstates of a system.
From Statistical Mechanics: S = kB ln Ω. Where: Ω= number of microstates, kB =
Boltzmann constant.
• Entropy is a state function and follows the 2nd Law of Thermodynamics:
∆Suniverse ≥0
• But note: entropy can decrease in part of a system (e.g., heat flow out), as long as the
total entropy of the universe increases.

Fb = ρliquid · Vdisplaced · g
where:

• Vdisplaced is the volume of fluid displaced (equal to the volume of the submerged object)

It increases with depth

• Archimedes’ Principle: Buoyant force equals the weight of the displaced fluid.

• Hydrostatic Pressure vs.
Buoyant Force: While pressure increases with depth,

• Equilibrium Path: Reversible transformations move through successive equilibrium
